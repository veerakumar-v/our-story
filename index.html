Right Person,
Wrong Direction

It started with a question in a ticket queue — and two people who somehow kept walking the wrong way toward each other.

Stop 01 · The Queue

A stranger, speaking first

She was a stranger, and she spoke first — right there in the ticket queue. She didn't know where to buy her ticket.

“This line,” I told her, pointing to where I stood. She smiled, and joined it.

Stop 02 · The First Feeling

Just imagination, I told myself

Something shifted in that first look — quiet, quick, easy to miss.

I told myself it was nothing. Just imagination, running ahead of itself.

Stop 03 · Walking Together

Walking like we weren't strangers

Ticket in hand, I waited for her. When she was done, we started walking — no plan, no particular hurry.

Just two strangers, walking like they weren't strangers at all.

Stop 04 · Opposite Directions

Same train, opposite ends

Same train, it turned out — but our coaches sat on opposite ends of the platform.

We said something like goodbye, and turned to go our separate ways.

Stop 05 · The Realization

I understood a second too late

The second she was out of sight, it hit me. That feeling wasn't ordinary — it was rare.

And it was already gone. I must have looked half out of my mind, standing there replaying the last ten minutes.

Stop 06 · Her Voice

Then I heard her voice

Then — her voice, behind me. I turned around, and she was walking back. Toward me, not away.

I don't think I've stopped hearing that voice since.

Stop 07 · Grandma's Call

A call from her grandmother

We fell into step again. A few paces in, her phone rang — her grandmother.

When the call ended, she realized something: she'd been walking the wrong way the whole time, even before we'd met.

Stop 08 · The Courage

Whatever courage I had

Before she could leave again, I found whatever courage I had and asked for her Instagram. She gave it to me.

Then she told me her name — and challenged me to spell it correctly. I tried. I failed. Instagram, at least, would teach me properly.

Stop 09 · Nine Friends, Zero Matches

Not one of them knew her

Phone still in hand, I checked every mutual connection I could think of — nine friends in total.

Not one of them knew her. Not one.

Stop 10 · The Mirrored Mistake

I'd been going the wrong way too

I called a friend, half-distracted — and that's when I realized it.

I'd been walking the wrong way too. Just like her. Neither of us had been looking where we were going.

Stop 11 · The Chase

Running before she was gone

Her family was close by, and once she reached them, this would be over. So I ran.

I found her. “What happened?” she asked, casual, like nothing was at stake. “Wrong direction,” I said. “Same as you. I think we were both too busy talking to notice.”

Stop 12 · The Coach Number

One last small thing to ask

She asked which coach I'd be in — one last small thing to hold onto — then turned and went with her family.

We parted quietly, without a word that felt big enough for what had just happened.

Stop 13 · On Board

Before my bag was even down

I found my seat, and before anything else, I found her account and followed it.

Then I started typing.

Stop 14 · To Be Continued

The train was already moving

If I want a follow back, I know what I have to do: something unmistakably me.

The train was already moving. So, it turns out, was I.
